# 6 Limitations

**Text alignment sits where CFG's FD optimum sits.** The guided rows score CLIP (100·cos) 29.4–29.6 at 16 px, where CFG at the training default $w = 4$ scores 30.06 (R@1 18 vs 13–15 %; seed 0), and the gap to the $w=4$ row grows to −0.9 at 32 px (Table 7); snapshot and label references agree within 0.2, the gap grows with $w$, and it is absent for reverse references. This is the CLIP level of CFG at $w \approx 1$–1.5 (29.43 / 29.74): along the CFG weight curve CLIP rises monotonically with $w$ while FD is U-shaped, so lowering CFG to its own FD optimum pays the same alignment as our rows, and the guided rows are 3–5 FD below the best CFG point and further below the CFG curve at their own CLIP (§4.9, Table 11, `fig_pareto_fd_clip.png`). The limitation is therefore not a cost peculiar to reference guidance but a shared frontier: recovering $w=4$-level CLIP (30.06) costs FD along the same frontier for everyone. Folding the CFG direction into the reference (`bucketu`) or adding a plain-CFG term (`cfg_text`, 3 NFE) reaches CLIP 29.65–29.89 at +0.9–4.4 FD over the composed row, none of seven variants reaches 30.06 (Table 8), and the exchange rate (≈ +0.5–1 FD per +0.1 CLIP) is gentler than CFG's own (+3 FD per 0.1 CLIP between $w = 1.5$ and 4) but not zero. `bucketu`-composed at $w = 1.5$ (FD 8.60, CLIP 29.77 = real-data level 29.80; R@1 16.1 % vs 16.4 %; 2 NFE) is an operating point that dominates the best CFG point (12.24, 29.74) in both coordinates, not a fix. CFG at $w=4$ scores *above* the real sprites (30.06 vs 29.80), so part of what it recovers is over-alignment; whether the difference between 29.5 and 30.06 is perceptible is not something our metrics answer. One gap remains: no human study distinguishes "aligned" from "over-aligned", so whether the best-CFG rows' CLIP excess over the real-data value at 20 / 24 px (+0.16 / +0.08) is a benefit or an artefact of CLIP-like text conditioning is not settled.

**The label-only gain shrinks with resolution and is partly metric-specific.** Under FD-DINOv2 the lower-bucket reference alone matches autoguidance at 16 px (−32 % vs best-CFG, −30 % for autoguidance), but its stand-alone advantage over a re-tuned CFG falls to −17 / −12 / −7 % at 20 / 24 / 32 px (autoguidance −20 / −20 / −10 %; composed −27 / −29 / −17 %; Table 3). Under Inception-v3 FID/KID on the same samples it still helps at 16 and 20 px but is flat or slightly worse at 24 and 32 px (16.13 → 16.45, KID 2.40 → 3.19; 19.77 → 20.10; bare rows at $w=4$) and lags autoguidance on the second model (9.77 vs 8.25). Consistently, 16-colour quantisation removes most of its advantage (Table 1; Table (g′)): the label reference mainly repairs per-pixel colour and contrast statistics, which DINOv2 (one patch per pixel) weights heavily and 64 px Inception barely sees. The composed reference and the reverse-control result hold under every metric and against best-CFG at every resolution, but the free, zero-storage variant is a 16 px result plus a component of the composed method — the part that removes the colour-domain mean shift — and should not be read as a stand-alone replacement for a snapshot at 20–32 px.

**Floor and headroom.** With 3,000 references the matched FD floor is 3.45 at 16 px and 12–14 at 20–32 px; best-CFG is 9.0 above the floor at 16 px but 27–70 above it at 20–32 px (the $w=4$ default: 18.5 and 34–83, seed 0), where training data is scarce. The remaining gap after guidance (7.5 vs 3.45) is real: guided samples still have about 1.8× the colour count and a quarter of the flat-region fraction of real sprites. Inception (floor 6.72 vs bare 9.57 at 16 px, $w=4$) has roughly 6.5× less headroom, so its relative gains are compressed.

**Coverage of buckets.** The method needs a lower bucket. At 12 px only snapshot autoguidance applies (best-CFG 9.71 at $w = 2$, seed 0 → 8.54 ± 0.73 over three seeds, −12 %; 13.02 ± 0.28 → 8.54, −34 %, against the $w=4$ default), and a trained contrast-shrunk label does not fill the gap (9.66 vs autoguidance 8.16, seed 0).

**Baseline tuning.** The CFG weight was tuned on the same held-out FD we report (sweep over $w \in \{1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 4, 7, 10\}$ at 16 px and $\{1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 4\}$ elsewhere; §4.3), as were our fixed $w = 2$ / $1.5$ and the choice of lower bucket at 24 px; there is no separate validation split. The sweep favours the baseline: the guided rows use one fixed $w$ per reference type at every resolution, whereas best-CFG is re-selected per resolution and per model, and the re-tuning alone accounts for −42 % at 16 px and −14…−16 % at 20–32 px of the gap to the $w=4$ default. The q16, Inception and pure-belief statistics of the best-CFG samples were not computed; those comparisons are against the $w=4$ default and are marked as such.

**Scope of the evidence.** Both models are our own, trained on one dataset (OGA-derived sprites, noisy BLIP captions) within one architecture family and sharing data and lineage, although v7s is independently and cleanly trained. We have not tested a public bucketed or micro-conditioned model (e.g. SDXL `original_size` conditioning at 256–512 px, Matryoshka, FiT [TODO cite]); generality beyond very-low-resolution pixel art is untested, as is the empirical relation to SDXL's `negative_original_size` practice. There is no human preference study yet; for a perceptual domain this is a genuine gap, and the FD–CLIP trade-off above is exactly what it should settle. A third model, v7_lowres, trained on data including the evaluation sprites, agrees with the clean models (16.66 → 7.40, seed 0) but is relegated to the appendix as contaminated. Finally, this is not a new architecture, and the trained degraded-view branches we tried do not beat the zero-training rule.

# Broader impact

The method is a sampling-time guidance rule for an existing class of generative models; it adds no data, capacity or capability and costs the same as CFG (Table 10). Its intended application, small-sprite generation for games and interfaces, carries the usual concerns of generative art tools: displacement of pixel artists and reproduction of styles in the training corpus. Our training data are OGA-derived sprites [TODO cite OGA; TBD: state licence terms]; the evaluation sprites are excluded from training. Because the gain is mainly in colour and contrast statistics rather than semantics, and text adherence sits at the level of CFG at its own FD optimum rather than above it, the method is unlikely to raise the risk of targeted misuse relative to the underlying model. If it transfers to large bucketed models it would remove the need to store a second network for guidance, a modest efficiency benefit rather than a new capability.
