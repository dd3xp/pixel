# 6 Limitations

**Text alignment is traded for distribution match.** Every reference-guidance row, autoguidance included, pays a small systematic text-alignment cost relative to bare CFG: about −0.5 CLIP (100·cos) and −4 % R@1 at 16 px (30.06 → 29.37–29.58; 18 → 13–15 %), growing to −0.9 CLIP at 32 px (Table 7). The cause is structural: a $w = 2$ same-caption reference term replaces the $w = 4$ unconditional CFG term; snapshot and label references agree within 0.2, the cost grows with $w$, and it is absent for reverse references. We found no free fix: folding the CFG direction into the reference (`bucketu`) or adding a plain-CFG term (`cfg_text`, 3 NFE) moves along an FD–CLIP frontier on which every +0.1 CLIP costs roughly +0.5–1 FD, and none of seven variants reaches the CLIP of bare CFG (Table 8). We therefore present `bucketu`-composed at $w = 1.5$ as an operating point, not a fix: real-data-level CLIP (29.77 vs 29.80; R@1 16.1 % vs 16.4 %) at +0.9 FD (8.60 vs 7.67) and unchanged cost. Bare CFG scores *above* the real sprites (30.06 vs 29.80), so part of the "cost" is the removal of CFG over-alignment; whether it is perceptible is not something our metrics answer.

**The label-only gain is partly metric-specific.** Under FD-DINOv2 the lower-bucket reference alone matches autoguidance at 16 px and helps at 20–32 px. Under Inception-v3 FID/KID on the same samples it still helps at 16 and 20 px but is flat or slightly worse at 24 and 32 px (16.13 → 16.45, KID 2.40 → 3.19; 19.77 → 20.10) and lags autoguidance on the second model (9.77 vs 8.25). Consistently, 16-colour quantisation removes most of its advantage (Table 1; Table (g′)): the label reference mainly repairs per-pixel colour and contrast statistics, which DINOv2 (one patch per pixel) weights heavily and 64 px Inception barely sees. The composed reference and the reverse-control result hold under every metric, but the label-only rows should be read as a colour-domain correction.

**Floor and headroom.** With 3,000 references the matched FD floor is 3.45 at 16 px and 12–14 at 20–32 px; the bare model is 18.5 above the floor at 16 px but 34–83 above it at 20–32 px, where training data is scarce. The remaining gap after guidance (7.5 vs 3.45) is real: guided samples still have about 1.8× the colour count and a quarter of the flat-region fraction of real sprites. Inception (floor 6.72 vs bare 9.57 at 16 px) has roughly 7× less headroom, so its relative gains are compressed.

**Coverage of buckets.** The method needs a lower bucket. At 12 px only snapshot autoguidance applies (13.02 ± 0.28 → 8.54 ± 0.73, three seeds), and a trained contrast-shrunk label does not fill the gap (9.66 vs autoguidance 8.16, seed 0).

**Scope of the evidence.** Both models are our own, trained on one dataset (OGA-derived sprites, noisy BLIP captions) within one architecture family and sharing data and lineage, although v7s is independently and cleanly trained. We have not tested a public bucketed or micro-conditioned model (e.g. SDXL `original_size` conditioning at 256–512 px, Matryoshka, FiT [TODO cite]); generality beyond very-low-resolution pixel art is untested, as is the empirical relation to SDXL's `negative_original_size` practice. There is no human preference study yet; for a perceptual domain this is a genuine gap, and the FD–CLIP trade-off above is exactly what it should settle. A third model, v7_lowres, trained on data including the evaluation sprites, agrees with the clean models (16.66 → 7.40) but is relegated to the appendix as contaminated. Finally, this is not a new architecture, and the trained degraded-view branches we tried do not beat the zero-training rule.

# Broader impact

The method is a sampling-time guidance rule for an existing class of generative models; it adds no data, capacity or capability and costs the same as CFG (Table 10). Its intended application, small-sprite generation for games and interfaces, carries the usual concerns of generative art tools: displacement of pixel artists and reproduction of styles in the training corpus. Our training data are OGA-derived sprites [TODO cite OGA; TBD: state licence terms]; the evaluation sprites are excluded from training. Because the gain is mainly in colour and contrast statistics rather than semantics, and text adherence is slightly weakened, the method is unlikely to raise the risk of targeted misuse relative to the underlying model. If it transfers to large bucketed models it would remove the need to store a second network for guidance, a modest efficiency benefit rather than a new capability.
