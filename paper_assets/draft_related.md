# 2 Related Work

**Guidance in diffusion models.** Classifier-free guidance (CFG, [cite: HoSalimans2022]) extrapolates the conditional prediction away from an unconditional one from the same network. Its failure modes, over-saturation and loss of diversity, have motivated variants that keep the unconditional reference but change how the difference is applied: guidance intervals restrict CFG to a middle range of noise levels [cite: Kynkaanniemi2024]; APG [cite: Sadat2024apg] removes the component of the update parallel to the conditional prediction and adds momentum; FDG [cite: Sabour2025] down-weights the low-frequency bands of a Laplacian pyramid; CADS [cite: Sadat2024cads] anneals noise into the condition embedding; feedback guidance [cite? Koulischer2025] and time-varying schedules [cite? TVCFG2025] adapt the weight per sample or step; CFG++ [cite: Chung2024] reinterprets it as a manifold-constrained interpolation. We tested APG, FDG, interval scheduling and CADS in our setting (Tables a″, e): none improves on the composed reference; APG and FDG remove exactly the radial, low-frequency component that we identify as the useful correction, and interval scheduling yields only a diagnostic (the snapshot's contribution lives in the low-noise half). We also sweep the CFG weight itself (§4.3, Table 2): the FD optimum is w = 1.5 at 16 px rather than the recipe default 4, and we compare against the per-resolution optimum throughout. Our rule changes the *reference*, not the weight or the application of the difference.

**Weak-model and self references.** Autoguidance [cite: Karras2024] replaces the unconditional reference by a smaller or less-trained version of the same model, arguing that the weak model must share the strong model's errors in amplified form (a "compatible degradation"); its ablations vary capacity and training time, not the conditioning label. It is our control and one half of the composed reference. Other works obtain a weak prediction without a second model: by blurring attention-selected input regions [cite: Hong2023], restricting the receptive field through crops [cite? SWG2024], sampling sub-networks [cite? S2Guidance2025], inference-time dropout (reported ineffective, [cite? InSituAG2025]), a random condition or perturbed time-step embedding (ICG/TSG, [cite: Sadat2024icg]), a degraded *text* condition (CDG, [cite? CDG2026]), or jointly trained intermediate-layer heads as low-frequency predictors [cite? SGGBR2026; cite? IG2025; cite? SSG2026]. Ours changes only the target-resolution label: same network, same input, same text. The distinction is empirical as well as conceptual. Degraded-input references fail in our regime (2 × 2 box-blurred x_t: FD 223.07; 1 px shift: 18.95); a trained block-average branch, the 16 px analogue of a low-frequency head, is harmful; and the reference is not random, since a lower label helps, a higher label hurts (Table f), and mixing the lower label with the unconditional prediction lands between the two (9.42 vs 8.59 and 21.98, seed 0).

**Perturbed self-attention.** Perturbed-Attention Guidance (PAG, [cite: Ahn2024], arXiv 2403.17377) and Smoothed Energy Guidance (SEG, [cite: Hong2024], arXiv 2408.00760) obtain a training-free weak prediction by replacing or blurring the self-attention map inside the network, at the same 2 NFE as CFG. They perturb the *computation*; we perturb only the *condition*. PAG is the most-used zero-training self-reference method and is our direct competitor; we compare against it in Table 2 and Table 11 (§4.3, §4.9). In our setting PAG in the mid block gives FD 12.57 / 12.62 / 12.90 at w = 1.5 / 2 / 3 (16 px, seed 0; CLIP 29.45 / 29.41 / 29.45), i.e. it tracks the best CFG point (12.24 at w = 1.5) as a gentler CFG that does not explode at w = 3, pays the same CLIP as CFG at that FD, and does not remove the systematic error that the lower-bucket reference removes (8.59 at the same seed); adding a CFG term (3 NFE) brings it to 11.73.

The closest prior trick is SDXL micro-conditioning [cite: Podell2023]. SDXL conditions the denoiser on the *original size* of each training image before resizing; in common practice [cite? diffusers] users pass a small `negative_original_size` to the negative branch of CFG, where it is documented to induce "simpler patterns" that CFG then steers away from. The relation should be stated precisely: there the reference is the CFG negative branch, so it also drops the text; the size label encodes the quality of the *source* image, not the target bucket; and the effect has, to our knowledge, not been quantified or tested for direction. It is nevertheless the closest prior use of a resolution embedding as a *negative* condition, and our `bucketu:12` rows (Appendix Table g″: lower bucket *and* empty caption in the weak branch) are its direct analogue in our model — they are 1.9–3.5 FD worse than keeping the caption fixed (10.45 / 12.10 vs 8.59, seed 0), and at w = 2 no better than the best CFG point (12.24). We keep the text fixed, use the label of a lower *target*-resolution bucket of the same network as the sole reference, show that it works as guidance at the cost of CFG, and give a mechanism (lower total variation with preserved structure) and a directional control (higher buckets hurt).

**Multi-resolution and bucketed diffusion.** Training one model over several resolutions or aspect ratios with a bucket label is standard practice, from aspect-ratio bucketing [cite? NovelAI2022] and SDXL's size and crop conditioning [cite: Podell2023] to Matryoshka diffusion, which denoises nested resolutions jointly [cite: Gu2023], FiT [cite: Lu2024], ScaleCrafter [cite: He2023] and the multi-scale losses of simple diffusion [cite: Hoogeboom2023]. These works provide the resolution embedding; none uses it as a guidance reference at sampling time. We show that it is a free contrast knob whose lower settings are compatible weak references.

**Pixel-art generation.** Most learned pixel-art work converts images to pixel art [cite: Gerstner2012; cite: Han2018; cite: Wu2022; cite? PixelOE2024]. Generative sprite models include GANs for character sprites [cite: Coutinho2022], fixed-palette index-map generators [cite? Merino2023] and diffusion models conditioned on a given palette [cite? PixDiffPIG2025] or on pose sheets [cite? SpriteSheetDiffusion2024]. SD-πXL [cite: Binninger2024] optimises a Gumbel-softmax palette assignment per image with score distillation from a large text-to-image model, at the cost of per-image optimisation. Colour bleeding is routinely handled by palette quantisation after generation; we report 16-colour quantisation as a secondary column, where it helps the bare model considerably (21.98 → 12.64 at 16 px for CFG at the w = 4 default) but not the guided rows, so part of our gain is in the colour domain and the two are not additive. We are not aware of prior work on guidance for diffusion at or below 32 px.

**Evaluating tiny images.** FID [cite: Heusel2017] and clean-FID [cite: Parmar2022] compute Inception features on natural-image-sized inputs, for which a 16 px sprite is a heavily upsampled block image; Stein et al. [cite: Stein2023] argue for DINOv2 features [cite: Oquab2023], under which each sprite pixel maps to exactly one patch. We use FD-DINOv2 with a matched protocol (one sample per held-out caption, a disjoint reference set of real sprites, a real-vs-real floor at every resolution) as the primary metric, with its mean/covariance decomposition, precision/recall [cite: Kynkaanniemi2019] and density/coverage [cite: Naeem2020] as diagnostics, Inception clean-FID and KID [cite: Binkowski2018] on the same samples as a second family, and CLIP similarity [cite: Radford2021] for text faithfulness. Inception has about 6.5× less headroom here (16 px baseline at w = 4: 9.57 against a floor of 6.72, versus 21.98 against 3.45 for DINOv2), yet rank agreement between the families is high (Spearman 0.86 at 16 px, n = 121), so the main claims do not depend on the feature extractor.
