# 1 Introduction

Pixel-art sprites are among the smallest images anyone draws on purpose: a character or item on a 12–32 px RGBA canvas, made of hard edges, a few flat colour regions and a transparent background. They are an unforgiving target for a generative model. There is no texture in which to hide a mistake, a single wrongly coloured pixel is visible, and the perceived quality of a sprite is carried almost entirely by per-pixel colour statistics and local contrast. The standard metric sees the same thing: when a 16 px sprite is upsampled with nearest-neighbour interpolation to the 224 px input of DINOv2 [cite: Oquab2023], every 14 × 14 patch covers exactly one sprite pixel, so the Fréchet distance in DINOv2 space (FD-DINOv2, [cite: Stein2023]) is a learned kernel on the 16 × 16 colour grid that penalises isolated wrong pixels and mixed colours heavily.

Measured this way, a well-trained text-to-sprite diffusion model is far from the data. Our clean 16 px baseline (a standard RGBA UNet with a frozen CLIP text encoder and a resolution-bucket class embedding, trained from scratch with the evaluation sprites excluded) reaches FD-DINOv2 21.98 under classifier-free guidance (CFG, [cite: HoSalimans2022]) at weight 4, whereas a second set of held-out real sprites scores 3.45. More than half of the gap (a mean term of 12.13) is a systematic shift of the feature mean. Visually the shift is colour bleeding: the denoiser regresses towards a mean, adjacent colours mix, local contrast is lost. CFG does not repair this; on the same model every stronger CFG weight, CADS [cite: Sadat2024cads] and a guidance interval [cite: Kynkaanniemi2024] make FD worse (Table a″). The problem is a systematic error of the denoiser rather than weak conditioning, which is the regime autoguidance [cite: Karras2024] targets. Autoguidance extrapolates the prediction away from that of a deliberately weaker, "compatibly degraded" version of the same model, and it works here: an EMA snapshot from step 10 k of the same run lowers FD from 21.52 ± 0.47 to 8.73 ± 0.26 over three seeds. It requires, however, a second set of weights to store and, more generally, a snapshot that may not exist.

Our observation is that a *bucketed* multi-resolution model already contains a compatible weak reference. Such models receive the target resolution as a label; ours is trained over buckets {12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 48, 64} px, with each sprite also seen as box-downsampled copies at lower buckets. If we give the same weights the same noisy input x_t and the same caption but the label of a *lower* bucket (12 when generating at 16), the output is not a smaller sprite. It is a prediction on the same 16 × 16 grid with the same layout and about the same number of colours as the strong prediction (78 vs 73 median unique colours), but with systematically lower local contrast: its mean adjacent-pixel difference (total variation, TV) is 21.6 against 32.0 for the strong prediction and 30.2 for real sprites. It is a structure-aligned, lower-contrast version of what the model believes, which is exactly the direction of the error we want to remove.

The method is a single line. For target bucket b_R and caption c, compute e_strong = ε_θ(x_t, t, c, b_R) and e_weak = ε_θ(x_t, t, c, b_low) with b_low < b_R, and denoise with e = e_weak + w (e_strong − e_weak), w ≈ 2. This *replaces* CFG, so each step still costs two network evaluations; no unconditional forward, no second model, no training. The weak forward can also be taken through an early snapshot under the lower label, composing both references at unchanged cost. Network, data and training recipe are standard and unchanged; this paper contributes a guidance rule and its analysis, not an architecture.

**Contributions.**

- **A zero-training guidance rule for bucketed multi-resolution diffusion models.** The model's own prediction under a lower bucket label as weak reference lowers 16 px FD-DINOv2 from 21.52 ± 0.47 (CFG, w = 4) to 8.52 ± 0.29 over three seeds, matching early-snapshot autoguidance (8.73 ± 0.26) without a snapshot. Composing the two references reaches 7.53 ± 0.19, a 65 % reduction, against a floor of 3.45. The label reference costs what CFG costs: two evaluations per step, 72.7 s vs 73.8 s per 1000 samples at identical peak memory.
- **Generality.** The ordering bare > label reference ≈ autoguidance > composed holds at every resolution with a lower bucket: 20 px (47.04 ± 1.11 → 32.75 ± 0.60 → 28.90 ± 0.69), 24 px (78.96 ± 0.74 → 59.32 ± 1.37 → 48.23 ± 0.43), 32 px (96.85 → 77.45 → 69.27), and on a clean second model with 41.3 M rather than 72.5 M parameters (28.00 → 12.80 → 10.54 at 16 px, −63 %). Under Inception clean-FID/KID the composed reference is best or tied-best at every resolution.
- **Directionality.** A *higher*-bucket reference is worse than no guidance at 12, 24 and 32 px (14.45 vs 12.91; 101.67 vs 79.80; 113.93 vs 96.85) and far weaker than the lower label at 16 px (17.14 vs 8.59). A wrong label is not a generic unconditional-like reference.
- **A mechanism with controlled trained ablations.** At three resolutions, every reference that helps has lower TV than the strong prediction while keeping its colour count and structure; every reference with TV at or above it is ineffective or harmful. The label is a monotone contrast knob (TV 21.6 → 31.9 → 40.9 for buckets 12 → 24 → 64). Low TV is not sufficient: a trained 2 × 2 block-average branch (TV 14.2, block structure) is harmful, and a trained contrast-shrunk branch (TV 15.9, structure-aligned) helps but is weaker than the free label under the same weights (14.71 vs 9.43). The label reference removes the mean shift, the snapshot restores coverage and acts only in the low-noise half of the trajectory.
- **Negative results.** APG, frequency-decoupled guidance, interval scheduling, CADS, stronger CFG, degraded-input references and channel-decoupled weights all fail to improve on the composed reference, consistently with a radial, low-frequency correction of per-image contrast.
- **An honest cost.** Every reference-based guidance, autoguidance included, pays a small text-alignment price relative to CFG at w = 4: CLIP similarity (100·cos) falls by about 0.5 at 16 px (30.06 → 29.51; retrieval R@1 18 % → 14 %) and by 0.9 at 32 px. A variant with the empty caption in the weak branch restores real-data-level alignment (29.77 vs 29.80) at +0.9 FD; we find no free fix.
