# 1 Introduction

Pixel-art sprites are among the smallest images anyone draws on purpose: a character or item on a 12–32 px RGBA canvas, made of hard edges, a few flat colour regions and a transparent background. They are an unforgiving target for a generative model. There is no texture in which to hide a mistake, a single wrongly coloured pixel is visible, and the perceived quality of a sprite is carried almost entirely by per-pixel colour statistics and local contrast. The standard metric sees the same thing: when a 16 px sprite is upsampled with nearest-neighbour interpolation to the 224 px input of DINOv2 [cite: Oquab2023], every 14 × 14 patch covers exactly one sprite pixel, so the Fréchet distance in DINOv2 space (FD-DINOv2, [cite: Stein2023]) is a learned kernel on the 16 × 16 colour grid that penalises isolated wrong pixels and mixed colours heavily.

Measured this way, a well-trained text-to-sprite diffusion model is far from the data. Our clean 16 px baseline (a standard RGBA UNet with a frozen CLIP text encoder and a resolution-bucket class embedding, trained from scratch with the evaluation sprites excluded) reaches FD-DINOv2 21.52 ± 0.47 (three seeds) under classifier-free guidance (CFG, [cite: HoSalimans2022]) at the training-recipe default weight w = 4, whereas a second set of held-out real sprites scores 3.45. The default is over-guided: sweeping w over {1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 7, 10} traces a U-shaped FD curve whose minimum is w = 1.5 at 12.49 ± 0.72 (three seeds; seed 0: 16.39 / 12.24 / 12.98 / 16.67 / 21.98 / 42.95 / 62.12), and no CFG weight reaches below 12.2 (Table 2). We therefore report every gain against this *best-CFG* baseline, re-tuned at each resolution, and give the w = 4 figure second. Even at its optimum CFG leaves a large systematic error: about half of the remaining FD (a mean term of 5.87 of 12.24 for seed 0; 12.13 of 21.98 at w = 4) is a shift of the feature mean, against 0.38 for real sprites. Visually the shift is colour bleeding: the denoiser regresses towards a mean, adjacent colours mix, local contrast is lost. Changing how CFG is applied does not repair it either: on the same model CADS [cite: Sadat2024cads] and a guidance interval [cite: Kynkaanniemi2024] are worse than plain CFG, and perturbed-attention guidance [cite: Ahn2024] is a gentler CFG (12.57–12.90 for w = 1.5–3) that never goes below the CFG optimum (Table 2). The problem is a systematic error of the denoiser rather than a mis-set guidance weight, which is the regime autoguidance [cite: Karras2024] targets. Autoguidance extrapolates the prediction away from that of a deliberately weaker, "compatibly degraded" version of the same model, and it works here: an EMA snapshot from step 10 k of the same run lowers FD to 8.73 ± 0.26 over three seeds, 30 % below best-CFG (59 % below the w = 4 default). It requires, however, a second set of weights to store and, more generally, a snapshot that may not exist.

Our observation is that a *bucketed* multi-resolution model already contains a compatible weak reference. Such models receive the target resolution as a label; ours is trained over buckets {12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 48, 64} px, with each sprite also seen as box-downsampled copies at lower buckets. If we give the same weights the same noisy input x_t and the same caption but the label of a *lower* bucket (12 when generating at 16), the output is not a smaller sprite. It is a prediction on the same 16 × 16 grid with the same layout and about the same number of colours as the strong prediction (78 vs 73 median unique colours), but with systematically lower local contrast: its mean adjacent-pixel difference (total variation, TV) is 21.6 against 32.0 for the strong prediction and 30.2 for real sprites. It is a structure-aligned, lower-contrast version of what the model believes, which is exactly the direction of the error we want to remove.

The method is a single line. For target bucket b_R and caption c, compute e_strong = ε_θ(x_t, t, c, b_R) and e_weak = ε_θ(x_t, t, c, b_low) with b_low < b_R, and denoise with e = e_weak + w (e_strong − e_weak), w ≈ 2. This *replaces* CFG, so each step still costs two network evaluations; no unconditional forward, no second model, no training. The weak forward can also be taken through an early snapshot under the lower label, composing both references at unchanged cost; this *composed* reference is the method we advertise, and the label alone is its free, zero-storage component. Network, data and training recipe are standard and unchanged; this paper contributes a guidance rule and its analysis, not an architecture.

**Contributions.**

- **A zero-training guidance rule for bucketed multi-resolution diffusion models, and its composition with autoguidance.** Evaluating an early snapshot under the lower bucket label — the *composed* reference, one weak forward, two evaluations per step like CFG — lowers 16 px FD-DINOv2 to 7.53 ± 0.19 over three seeds, 40 % below the best CFG weight (12.49 ± 0.72 at w = 1.5, swept per resolution; 65 % below the training default w = 4, 21.52 ± 0.47), below every seed of either single reference, against a floor of 3.45. The *label-only* reference — the model's own prediction under a lower bucket, same weights, same x_t, same caption — reaches 8.52 ± 0.29, 32 % below best-CFG (60 % below w = 4), matching early-snapshot autoguidance (8.73 ± 0.26, −30 %) with no snapshot stored; it costs what CFG costs: 72.7 s vs 73.8 s per 1000 samples at identical peak memory.
- **Generality.** Against best-CFG re-tuned at each resolution (w = 2.5 at 20 px, 2 at 24 and 32 px) the composed reference gains −27 % at 20 px (39.57 ± 0.04 → 28.90 ± 0.69), −29 % at 24 px (67.77 ± 0.77 → 48.23 ± 0.43) and −17 % at 32 px (83.65 → 69.27, seed 0); against the w = 4 default the gains are −39 / −39 / −28 % (47.04 ± 1.11, 78.96 ± 0.74, 96.85). On a clean second model with 41.3 M rather than 72.5 M parameters it gains −45 / −35 / −36 % at 16 / 20 / 24 px vs best-CFG (19.10 → 10.54, 56.30 → 36.31, 93.78 → 60.20, seed 0; −62 / −43 / −39 % vs w = 4). The ordering bare > label reference ≈ autoguidance > composed holds at every resolution with a lower bucket, but the label-only gain shrinks with resolution: −32 % vs best-CFG at 16 px, then −17 / −12 / −7 % at 20 / 24 / 32 px (autoguidance −30 / −20 / −20 / −10 %). The free variant is thus a 16 px result and a component of the composed method, not a stand-alone replacement for a snapshot at higher resolutions. Under Inception clean-FID/KID the composed reference is best or tied-best at every resolution.
- **Directionality.** A *higher*-bucket reference is worse than CFG at the training default at 12, 20, 24 and 32 px (seed-0 reverse rows 14.45, 59.13 for bucket:32, 101.67 and 113.93 against bare 13.02 ± 0.28, 47.04 ± 1.11, 78.96 ± 0.74 and 96.85 (seed 0); at 20 px bucket:24 gives 46.62, indistinguishable from bare) and worse than best-CFG at every resolution, 16 px included (17.14 vs 12.49 ± 0.72, seed 0, where the lower label gives 8.52 ± 0.29). A wrong label is not a generic unconditional-like reference.
- **A mechanism with controlled trained ablations and an interventional control.** At three resolutions, every reference that helps has lower TV than the strong prediction while keeping its colour count and structure; every reference with TV at or above it is ineffective or harmful. The label is a monotone contrast knob (TV 21.6 → 31.9 → 40.9 for buckets 12 → 24 → 64). Low TV is not sufficient: a trained 2 × 2 block-average branch (TV 14.2, block structure) is harmful, and a trained contrast-shrunk branch (TV 15.9, structure-aligned) helps but is weaker than the free label under the same weights (14.71 vs 9.43). Nor is the reference a low-contrast *scalar*: using the strong model's own prediction with contrast shrunk towards its per-image mean as the reference — which makes guidance a uniform contrast amplification, at 1 NFE — is monotonically harmful (FD 74.8 / 200.4 / 374.6 for shrink factors 0.85 / 0.7 / 0.5 at w = 2), so the lower-bucket belief's error direction is spatially structured. The label reference removes the mean shift, the snapshot restores coverage and acts only in the low-noise half of the trajectory.
- **Negative results.** APG, frequency-decoupled guidance, interval scheduling, CADS, CFG at any weight, perturbed-attention guidance, degraded-input references and channel-decoupled weights all fail to improve on the composed reference, consistently with a radial, low-frequency correction of per-image contrast.
- **The alignment–fidelity frontier.** The guided rows sit at CLIP similarity (100·cos) 29.4–29.6 at 16 px, where CFG at w = 4 scores 30.06 — but CFG pays the same: CLIP rises monotonically with w (29.43 → 30.06 for w = 1 → 4) while FD traces a U, so lowering CFG to its FD optimum w = 1.5 lands at CLIP 29.74. On the FD–CLIP plane every guided row lies 3–5 FD below the CFG curve at the same CLIP (`fig_pareto_fd_clip.png`), and two variants — the composed reference with the empty caption in the weak branch (8.60, CLIP 29.77 = real-data level 29.80) and composed plus a CFG term (9.19, 29.72; 3 NFE) — dominate the best CFG point (12.24, 29.74). Recovering the w = 4 CLIP of 30.06 costs FD along the same frontier for every method; we do not claim otherwise.
